## CONTENT

### 1. STRATEGIC FRAMING

#### Problem
Fashion e-commerce lives or dies on the moment a shopper goes from "I love this" to "where's the buy button?" The platform had problems:

- **Generic, template-driven design** — the storefront didn't feel like the brand; identity was lost in e-commerce defaults
- **Friction in browse-to-buy** — too many steps between inspiration and checkout
- **Catalog overload** — shoppers overwhelmed; no smart path from desire to product
- **Mobile-first collapse** — desktop "lookbook" layouts failed on phones where most shopping happened
- **Brand vs. conversion tension** — the more "fashion" it looked, the less it converted

The real problem wasn't "prettier product pages." It was: **how do you make a shopping experience that feels like the brand and still converts like a machine?**

#### Vision
> **A storefront that feels editorial, but converts with discipline.**

Fashion e-commerce where every screen carries brand energy — while the path from inspiration to purchase stays short, clear, and mobile-perfect.

### 2. METHODOLOGY — HOW I THINK

#### Research & Discovery
**Methods:** Brand audit (identity, tone), UX audit (existing storefront), Shopper interviews, Competitive analysis (premium DTC fashion), Funnel analytics review, Usability testing

**Participants:** [VALIDAR — shoppers, brand stakeholders]

**Duration:** [VALIDAR]

**Key Findings:**
1. **Shoppers buy the mood first, product second.** The site's energy matched the product — when it felt generic, trust dropped.
2. **Mobile was the front door, but the design was desktop-first.** Lookbook layouts collapsed into confusing stacks on phones.
3. **The buy button had a visibility problem.** In "fashion" designs, CTAs got buried under editorial styling.
4. **Catalog depth without guidance = abandonment.** Too many options with no path = decision paralysis.

**Artifacts:** Brand-style audit, Shopper journey map, Funnel drop-off analysis, Design principles workshop notes

#### Strategy & Framing
**Problem Statement (HMW):**
> How might we build a fashion storefront that carries brand energy on every screen while keeping the path from desire to checkout short and obvious?

**Design Principles:**
1. **Editorial energy, conversion discipline** — beauty serves the buy
2. **Mobile is the truth** — design for the phone first
3. **Show the desire path** — clear, guided route from inspiration to product
4. **CTA always wins the hierarchy** — the buy button never hides
5. **Brand, not template** — every surface carries identity

**Success Criteria:**
- Checkout reached from any product screen in ≤ 2 taps
- Mobile conversion improved against baseline
- Shoppers rate the experience as "on-brand"
- Reduced funnel drop-off

**Constraints & Tradeoffs:**

| Type | Description | Impact | How Addressed |
|------|-------------|--------|---------------|
| Brand | Editorial standards vs. conversion patterns | High | Hybrid layouts: editorial modules on conversion spine |
| Business | Existing platform constraints | Medium | Progressive redesign within the platform |
| Product | Catalog breadth vs. focus | Medium | Curated collection paths; smart defaults |
| Deadline | Design-to-dev handoff pressure | Medium | Tight design system + documented specs |

#### Design & Iteration
**Exploration:** Attempt 1 was pure editorial lookbook — beautiful, but funnel analytics showed drop-off at product pages (CTA hidden). Attempt 2 merged editorial photography with an always-visible conversion layer.

**Prototyping:** Lookbook-to-shop flow, Mobile-first PDP with sticky CTA, Collection paths with intent, Reduced-step checkout.

**Testing:** Usability tests (browse → cart → checkout), A/B on CTA placement, mobile-first validation.

**Key Decisions:**

| # | Context | Options | Decision | Rationale | Tradeoffs |
|---|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Visual approach | Pure editorial / Pure conversion / Hybrid | **Hybrid** | Brand energy + conversion spine | More complex to maintain |
| 2 | Mobile strategy | Responsive desktop / Mobile-first | **Mobile-first** | Most shoppers arrive on phones | Desktop needs a second pass |
| 3 | CTA style | Minimal fashion CTA / Always-visible sticky CTA | **Sticky always-visible CTA** | The buy button never hides | Slightly less "minimal" |
| 4 | Catalog presentation | Full grid / Curated paths / Both | **Curated paths + full grid** | Guidance reduces paralysis | Curation effort |

**Pivots:**
1. **Pure editorial → Hybrid** — Trigger: funnel drop-off at product pages. Learning: editorial beauty must never fight the buy.

#### Collaboration & Alignment
**Stakeholders:**
- Brand (marketing/creative) — Owns identity — Creative guardrails
- E-commerce team — Conversion targets — Business owners
- Engineering — Platform constraints — Builders
- Shoppers — End users — Adopters

**Alignment Story:**
Marketing wanted "a magazine." The e-commerce lead wanted "conversion at all costs." Both sides treated the other's goal as the enemy. The resolution was a hybrid spine: editorial photography and storytelling in the top of the journey, conversion discipline (sticky CTA, reduced steps, clear hierarchy) in the lower half. One visual language, two jobs — and funnel analytics proved both could live on the same screen.

**Handoff:** Mobile-first design system, PDP/PLP/checkout specs, CTA hierarchy guidelines, Editorial module library for marketing.

### 5. REFLECTION

#### Learnings

| Category | Insight | Application Today |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| Product | Editorial and conversion are not enemies — hybrid layouts serve both | Design the "spine" (conversion) and "skin" (brand) together |
| Product | CTA visibility beats styling every time — fashion CTA minimalism loses sales | Never let hierarchy bury the buy button |
| Process | Funnel analytics drove the pivot — the drop-off report settled a creative argument | Bring data into creative reviews |
| Product | Mobile-first is the only truth for retail | Phone-first, always |

#### What I'd Do Differently
1. **Joint creative+analytics kickoff** — Marketing and the e-commerce team aligned late; a shared funnel dashboard from day one would have avoided the "magazine vs. machine" framing.
2. **A/B the editorial modules earlier** — The hybrid was validated late; testing individual modules (hero, lookbook, PDP) separately would have told us which brand elements converted.
3. **Curate the catalog with merchandising** — Curated paths arrived after launch; merchandising-driven curation from the start would have reduced initial drop-off.
