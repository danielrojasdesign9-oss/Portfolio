## CONTENT

### 1. STRATEGIC FRAMING

#### Problem
I was terrible at expressing myself. I'd either:
- **Aggressive**: Say what I wanted, but hurt people in the process
- **Passive**: Keep my needs hidden, then resent others for not reading my mind
- **Passive-aggressive**: The worst of both worlds — resentful AND hurtful

**The core issue**: I had no framework for communication. I was winging it every time, and it showed.

The real problem wasn't "learn to talk better." It was: **how do you practice having hard conversations safely, without real-world damage?**

#### Vision
> **A personal communication coach that helps you say what you mean without being a jerk.**

An app built around two proven frameworks — DEAR MAN (for getting what you want) and CNV (for expressing feelings without blame) — that turns abstract communication theory into actionable practice.

### 2. METHODOLOGY — HOW I THINK

#### Research & Discovery
**Methods:** Personal pain documentation (conversation failures), Literature review (DEAR MAN from DBT, CNV from Rosenberg), Framework comparison, Self-reflection audits

**Participants:** Self + [VALIDAR — early testers]

**Duration:** [VALIDAR]

**Key Findings:**
1. **I kept messing up conversations with my partner.** I'd say something, see the hurt on their face, and think "that's not what I meant."
2. **DEAR MAN and CNV are the most practical frameworks** — from DBT and Rosenberg respectively. Academic concepts, but extremely actionable if translated well.
3. **The design challenge**: How do you make communication practice feel safe, not embarrassing?
4. **People learn better when they don't feel evaluated.** Scores ("you got 3/10 on empathy") create defensiveness, not growth.

**Artifacts:** Personal conversation failure log, Framework comparison matrix, Non-judgmental UX principles, Practice scenario library draft

#### Strategy & Framing
**Problem Statement (HMW):**
> How might we turn abstract communication theory into a safe, judgment-free practice space — so people can build assertive communication skills without real-world consequences?

**Design Principles:**
1. **Non-judgment is powerful** — No scores, no good/bad. Reflection over evaluation.
2. **Frameworks make communication less scary** — Structure beats winging it
3. **Safety first** — Practice without real-world damage
4. **Progress is non-linear** — Some days are better; that's okay
5. **Design from pain** — Solve your own problem first

**Success Criteria:**
- Users recognize their communication patterns (self-awareness)
- Practice feels safe, not embarrassing
- DEAR MAN/CNV understandable for non-academics
- Personal: conversations with partner move from fights to discussions

**Constraints & Tradeoffs:**

| Type | Description | Impact | How Addressed |
|------|-------------|--------|---------------|
| Personal | Highly personal/sensitive subject matter | High | Non-judgmental tone; no scores; reflection prompts |
| Technical | Solo project scope constraints | Medium | Local storage; scope-limited features |
| Product | Abstract frameworks → actionable practice | High | Step-by-step translation; realistic scenarios |
| Emotional | Practice can trigger discomfort | Medium | Safe framing; optional privacy; no pressure |

#### Design & Iteration
**Exploration:** Read every book on assertive communication. DEAR MAN and CNV stood out. The design challenge was translation: turning academic frameworks into steps that don't feel like homework.

**Prototyping:** DEAR MAN guided steps (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, Mindful, Appear confident, Negotiate), CNV reflection prompts (Observation, Feeling, Need, Request), Practice scenarios with branches, Subtle progress indicators.

**Testing:** Self-testing + [VALIDAR — small test group]. Measured: did practice feel safe? Did users recognize patterns? Did steps feel actionable?

**Key Decisions:**

| # | Context | Options | Decision | Rationale | Tradeoffs |
|---|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Framework focus | DEAR MAN only / CNV only / Both | **Both** | DEAR MAN for getting wants; CNV for expressing feelings without blame | More content to design; richer toolkit |
| 2 | Feedback model | Scores/evaluation / Reflection prompts / Both | **Reflection prompts** | Scores create defensiveness; reflection creates understanding | Less "measurable" progress |
| 3 | Practice format | Chatbot simulations / Scenario walkthroughs / Free-form | **Scenario walkthroughs** | Realistic, safe, structured | Less life-like than chatbot |
| 4 | Progress display | Grades/levels / Subtle indicators / Hidden | **Subtle indicators** | Encouragement without pressure | Progress less explicit |

**Pivots:**
1. **Evaluation → Reflection** — Trigger: instinct to score empathy felt wrong; testing confirmed scores create defensiveness. Learning: in sensitive domains, understanding beats measurement.

#### Collaboration & Alignment
**Stakeholders:**
- Self (primary user) — Designer, developer, user
- Partner — Implicit stakeholder, real-world validator
- Early testers — Feedback, validation

**Alignment Story:**
This was a solo project born from personal pain — repeated failed conversations with my partner. The frameworks were the lifeline. The hardest design decision was removing scores. Every instinct in me wanted "progress = number." But every time I imagined "you scored 4/10 on assertiveness" I cringed — and testing confirmed others felt the same. Reflection prompts, not scores, became the core mechanic. The measure of success wasn't a chart going up; it was my partner and I having actual discussions where we both felt heard.

**Handoff:** Framework translation guidelines, Scenario library, Reflection prompt system, Progress indicator specs.

### 5. REFLECTION

#### Learnings

| Category | Insight | Application Today |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| Process | Design from pain — the best projects solve YOUR problem first | Start with the problem you know deeply |
| Product | Non-judgment is powerful — people learn better when they don't feel evaluated | Remove evaluation from learning experiences |
| Product | Frameworks help — structure makes communication less scary | Translating expert frameworks into steps creates confident users |
| Product | Progress isn't linear — some days are better than others | Design for wobble, not just upward trends |
| Career | Personal projects matter — they push you to learn things you'd never explore at work | Keep making things for yourself |

#### What I'd Do Differently
1. **Recruit testers earlier** — Validating the no-scores decision would have come faster with a small feedback group.
2. **Add a structured self-reflection journal** — Reflection prompts inside scenarios were useful; a journal tying patterns across practices was missing.
3. **Ship the scenario editor** — Users (including me) hit the limits of pre-scripted scenarios. Letting users import their own real situations would have made the tool more durable.
